# 2026-10-13 | Week 8 | Group reflection and final deliverable

## Reflecting and putting the slides together

Today our group came together to reflect on the project and work on the final deliverable slides together, alongside our individual journal entries. Putting the presentation together gave us a chance to look back at how the project developed, from learning the basics of computer vision to getting three people to control the elephant and building out DigiPhant Kart.

Weeks 5 and 6 were mainly separate projects and exercises to learn the basics of computer vision before applying that learning to DigiPhant. Looking back across those weeks helped connect the early experiments with the decisions we made in the group project.

## Inspiration from other teams

Some teams presented custom interfaces for their cameras and loading screens. We took inspiration from how they made those parts of the experience feel more considered. It's something we'll keep in mind for future projects: the camera interface and loading screens are also opportunities to communicate how the experience works and give it a consistent identity.

## System analysis 1: three players, one kart

![System diagram showing webcam capture, local tracking and calibration, three player inputs, Unity actions, and feedback returning to the players](../assets/digiphant/system-player-input-feedback.png)

*Extracted from group process PDF, p. 6; diagram prepared October 13, 2026.*

The diagram explains the complete input-and-feedback loop. A webcam captures three people in separate regions. Local tracking and neutral calibration turn their movements into independent control signals. Unity combines those signals into one kart's behavior, and its animation, captions, sounds, and music help the performers adjust their next movements.

| Role | October 13 documented mapping |
| --- | --- |
| P1 / body | Left hand accelerates; right hand brakes or reverses |
| P2 / shoulders | Left shoulder turns right; right shoulder turns left |
| P3 / hand | Peace sign requests a jump; fist crouches; pinch requests a throw |

The conditions matter as much as the gestures: missing tracking can hold driving or disable the affected action; a throw needs a collected orange; fingers must relax between peace-sign jumps; changed camera settings require neutral recalibration. These dependencies explain why a visible gesture does not always produce an action.

This is the October 13 documented mapping. The earlier Week 7 text describes a different jump assignment and remains useful evidence of iteration.

## System analysis 2: lap progression and game state

![State-flow diagram showing start, repeated lap play, changing hazards on laps two and three, and race-finish feedback](../assets/digiphant/system-lap-progression.png)

*Extracted from group process PDF, p. 7; diagram prepared October 13, 2026.*

The second diagram explains how completing a lap changes the next state of the game. Restart begins with lap one and empty orange storage. Completing lap one enters lap two with two logs, two canopies, and six banana peels. Completing lap two enters lap three with three logs, three canopies, and twelve banana peels. The music becomes faster with progression. Finishing lap three leads to the victory cue and banner while race music fades.

During play, orange storage is capped at three and each throw consumes one. Banana contact causes a short slowdown and visual feedback. Newly added hazards wait when a racer is too near their spawn location. Restart restores the first-lap setup. This shows the relationships between events, state changes, and feedback rather than just the appearance of the track.
