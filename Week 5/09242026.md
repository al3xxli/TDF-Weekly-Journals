# Object Detection

![09242026-01](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/al3xxli/TDF-Weekly-Journals/main/embeds/09242026-01.png)

In frameworks, we just read about problem spaces, and the concept of a feature space is the same thing for object detection, it's a satisficing process that searches for all possible alternatives to what the object is where 1.00 confidence score is impossible.

Like we learned last class, images are broken down into patches of tokens. There's a mathematical function that determines what falls within and outside of the bounding box based on the object/confidence score. The bounding boxes are determined by IoU's (Intersection of Union) that will discard the weaker scoring box. This could help improve accuracy **but** can also create mistakes. For example, if an image as a dog in front of a person, the IoU may fail to detect the dog (weaker bounding box) and only label the images as a human (stronger bounding box).

As with everything, good training data is required. The more challenging a picture is (properly labelled), the more robust the model will become. Fun fact: CAPTCHA is the largest object training company because they take "correct" responses from humans and uses it to train AI models (free labor!).

# Pose Estimation

![09242026-02](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/al3xxli/TDF-Weekly-Journals/main/embeds/09242026-02.png)

Object detection runs first, to identify people in an image. Then the goal is to segment each person into separate joints as node labels, and then connecting them together with predefined edges to create the digital skeleton for the final pose. Essentially, it's like many sub-processes of object detection that each are trying to find in relation to each other (shoulders should always be below the head, etc). Pose estimation will also try to predict the location of hidden joints if something is covered or hidden in the photo based on its relation to known joints.
